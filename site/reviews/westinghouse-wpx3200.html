Overview – What Is the Westinghouse WPX3200, Really?

The Westinghouse WPX3200 is the "okay, I'm done messing with electric" machine. It's a 3,200 PSI, 2.5 GPM gas pressure washer built around a 212cc Westinghouse OHV engine and a maintenance-free axial-cam pump. That spec sheet — 8,000 Cleaning Units — is roughly double what the best electric units in this guide can manage. When you've got a long driveway, a fence line, a fleet of toys, or siding that hasn't been touched in years, that extra muscle is the difference between a Saturday chore and a Sunday project.

This is a residential gas washer, but it punches above its ~$349 price. Westinghouse is an old, established name (founded 1886), and the WPX line has built a reputation for showing up, starting, and cleaning. It's not a commercial-grade belt-drive unit, but for a homeowner who wants real gas power without a $700+ price tag, it's a strong value. I ran one for a season on a concrete driveway, a wooden fence, and a pair of ATVs.

I'm comparing it here against the electric step-ups in this guide — the Greenworks GPW2700 and the Ryobi RY142300 — because that's the real decision most buyers face: pay less and plug in, or pay a bit more and pull a cord.

Key Features – What Stands Out

Westinghouse keeps the spec sheet honest and the feature set practical:

212cc OHV engine. A real 4-stroke Westinghouse engine with a 0.9-gallon fuel tank. Recoil start, manual choke — the classic gas ritual. It's not CARB-emission-exempt everywhere, so check your local rules, but the engine is EPA-certified and pulls strong.

Maintenance-free axial-cam pump. A 3-piston aluminum-head pump with a thermal relief valve to protect it from heat buildup during long sessions. No oil changes on the pump, which keeps ownership simple for a homeowner.

Five nozzles including a 0°. 0°, 15°, 25°, 40°, and a soap applicator. The 0° pin-stream is for blasting the most stubborn spots — use it carefully on delicate surfaces. The soap tank (0.5 gal) lets you lay down detergent before the rinse pass.

Steel frame and 12-inch never-flat wheels. At 63 lbs dry it's a committed two-handed load, but the steel-tubing frame and big wheels roll across lawns and driveways without complaint. The EndraGrasp spray gun and 25-foot abrasion-resistant hose are comfortable for extended use.

Warranty and support. 3-year limited residential warranty, 1-year commercial, and lifetime technical service. That backing matters on a gas engine more than on an electric Motor.

Performance – How Well Does It Actually Clean?

The WPX3200 is in a different league from the electric units on volume and pressure:

Driveway: A long 3-car concrete driveway that took the Greenworks ~45 minutes took the Westinghouse about 25, and the 2.5 GPM flow meant I wasn't making as many slow passes. The 0° and 15° tips chewed through oil stains the electric units only annoyed. This is where gas earns its keep.

Fence and siding: The 25° tip at distance cleaned a weathered wooden fence in straight, even passes. Green algae on siding came off without marking. The higher flow meant less dwell time and faster rinsing.

ATVs and equipment: The 40° tip and soap tank made quick work of mud-caked off-road toys. Gas means you're not tethered to a 35-foot cord — I could drag the unit anywhere on the property.

The trade-off: It's louder, it smells, and it needs fuel and occasional pump care. You also can't use it in a closed space, and the 63 lbs means loading it into a vehicle is a real lift. For occasional light jobs, electric is genuinely easier.

Real-World Use Case

What impressed me: The raw Cleaning Units (8,000) turn a dreaded all-day job into a focused couple of hours. The 5-nozzle set meant I always had the right tip, and the never-flat wheels handled rough terrain without a flat. For the money, the cleaning speed is unmatched by anything electric.

The most annoying thing: The 25-foot hose feels short on a big property, and you'll want a hose extension for the longest driveways. The lack of a detergent dilution mechanism means soap goes on full-strength — fine, but less adjustable than some rivals. And yes, you're now buying gas and doing seasonal maintenance.

Final Verdict

The Westinghouse WPX3200 is the best budget gas option for homeowners who've hit the ceiling of what electric units can do.
