Overview – What Is the Greenworks GPW2700, Really?

The Greenworks GPW2700 is the machine you buy when the budget Sun Joe SPX3000 leaves you wanting more punch. It pushes 2,700 PSI — roughly a third more pressure than the SPX3000's 2,030 — and it does it with a brushless motor and a 10-year motor warranty that nobody else in this price bracket comes close to matching. Greenworks markets it as "Better Than Gas," and while that's marketing talk, the substance is real: no fumes, no oil changes, no pull-cord, and a continuous-run brushless motor that behaves like a gas unit the moment you squeeze the trigger.

This is a homeowner machine, but it's aimed at the slightly more serious homeowner. The one who has a long driveway, a two-story facade, or a deck that actually sees weather. It weighs about 50 pounds assembled versus the SPX3000's 24, so it's not a feather. But that extra heft buys you a steel wheelbarrow frame, 10-inch never-flat wheels, and a vertical-storage design that eats 50% less garage space. If you've ever tripped over a pressure washer in the corner of the shed, that storage trick alone is worth something.

I tested the GPW2700 over two weekends: once on my own concrete driveway and once helping a friend clean algae off a north-facing vinyl wall and a wooden fence. I've also owned the SPX3000 and the Ryobi RY142300, so I can place this unit directly against its two closest electric rivals.

Key Features – What Stands Out

Greenworks packs a lot into a box that retails around $399–$449. Here's what actually matters:

14-amp TruBrushless motor. The smart-sensing brushless motor automatically adjusts pressure and flow to whatever nozzle you've clicked in. That means the turbo tip gets more aggression and the 40° tip gets a gentler, wider fan without you fiddling with settings. Brushless also means longer motor life and less heat. The 10-year motor warranty is the headline — it's the best in the category by a mile.

Continuous-run pump. Unlike cheaper electric units that start and stop with the trigger, the GPW2700 runs like a gas machine: the motor and pump stay live while the unit is on, and a thermal relief valve dumps hot water from the pump to keep it cool during long jobs. You get instant, zero-lag pressure every time you pull the trigger.

Big onboard soap tank. A 5-liter (about 1.3-gallon) detergent reservoir sits on the frame. That's roughly double what most rivals carry. For car washing and siding prep, you'll rarely refill mid-job.

Five nozzles including a turbo. 15°, 25°, 40°, a soap nozzle, and a turbo nozzle that concentrates the stream for ~50% faster cleaning on stubborn concrete. The kink-resistant 25-foot hose and 35-foot GFCI power cord are both adequate for a typical suburban setup.

Certified and quiet-ish. It carries PWMA/CETA cleaning-equipment certification and runs at about 82 dB — loud enough to hear, quiet enough not to murder your Saturday-morning neighbor relations.

Performance – How Well Does It Actually Clean?

I ran the GPW2700 on four surfaces and compared the feel against the SPX3000 I used to own:

Driveway: This is where the extra 670 PSI shows up. With the turbo nozzle on a 2-car asphalt driveway, oil spots and ground-in dirt lifted in roughly 25 minutes — about 20 minutes faster than the same job on the SPX3000. The 1.2 GPM rated flow is modest (you'll see up to 2.3 GPM at low pressure), so it's not a firehose, but the pressure does the heavy lifting. Cleaning Units land at 3,240, which is genuinely strong for an electric.

Car washing: The 40° tip plus the big soap tank made a clean, low-risk wash. Soap application was generous, and rinsing stripped bug splatter off the front bumper without me leaning in. No foam cannon included, so thick detailing foam needs a separate accessory.

Vinyl siding and fence: The 25° tip at about 12 inches cleaned green algae off a north wall cleanly and took moss off a wooden fence that the old SPX3000 had only annoyed. Two years of buildup came off with steady, slow passes.

Reach: The 35-foot cord plus 25-foot hose let me clean a two-story gable from the ground by angling the turbo tip upward — no ladder required for the lower 20 feet. That's a real safety win over shorter-cord units.

Real-World Use Case

What impressed me: The continuous-run brushless motor means there is no "spool-up" delay — you pull the trigger and you've got full 2,700 PSI immediately. Combined with the oversized soap tank and the vertical-storage frame, this felt like the most "finished" electric washer I've used. The 10-year motor warranty is the clincher for peace of mind.

The most annoying thing: At ~50 lbs it's a two-handed load to lift into a trunk, and the 25-foot hose, while kink-resistant, still had me repositioning the unit on a long driveway. For the money I'd have liked a 30- or 35-foot hose.

Who Should Buy the Greenworks GPW2700?

  • Homeowners with a long or 2-car driveway
  • People who want gas-like power without gas maintenance
  • Buyers who value a long motor warranty
  • Anyone short on garage space (vertical storage)
  • Those cleaning siding, fences, and decks a few times a year

Who Should Skip It?

  • Strict budget shoppers (the SPX3000 is half the price)
  • Apartment dwellers who need a light, stowable unit
  • People who only wash a car occasionally
  • Users wanting maximum water volume (look at 2.5+ GPM gas)

Alternatives to Consider

If the GPW2700's weight or price doesn't fit, here are three alternatives:

1. Ryobi RY142300 (~$249) Lighter at 49 lbs and cheaper, with a 13-amp brushless motor and 2,300 PSI. Less pressure than the Greenworks but a better value if you don't need the extra punch.

2. Sun Joe SPX3000 (~$149) The budget king. 2,030 PSI and dual detergent tanks at half the price, but a shorter warranty and less muscle on concrete.

3. Westinghouse WPX3200 (~$349, gas) If you're willing to handle fuel, this 3,200 PSI / 2.5 GPM gas unit doubles the Cleaning Units for similar money — better for serious driveways and fleet cleaning.

Final Verdict

The Greenworks GPW2700 is a step-up electric for homeowners who've outgrown the budget class but don't want the hassle of gas. The 2,700 PSI brushless motor, continuous-run pump, and industry-leading 10-year motor warranty make it the most "buy-it-once" electric in its range. At ~$399–$449 it costs more than a Sun Joe, but you get noticeably more cleaning power and far longer peace of mind.

It's heavier and the hose could be longer, but for most houses — driveway, siding, fence, car — it delivers gas-adjacent results without the fumes, the oil, or the pull cord. Just budget a little garage space and maybe a hose extension for the longest jobs.
